<p class="lead">Post one test token as collateral and borrow another. If the price falls, does the protocol take the collateral by itself? A saved local run says no. Collateral moves only when someone sends a <Term id="liquidation">liquidation</Term> transaction.</p>

## Start with an everyday picture

Think of a pawn shop. You leave a watch and borrow cash. The watch’s quoted price falls. The shop’s coverage ratio gets worse. The watch is still in the cupboard.

The price quote does not lift the watch out. Someone has to follow the rule: repay part of the debt and take part of the collateral. Until that step, the watch is still the one you pledged.

On chain, that coverage ratio is the <Term id="health-factor">health factor</Term>. In plain words:

**collateral value × liquidation threshold ÷ debt**

Above 1, nobody may liquidate the position. The ratio must be **below** 1. A value of exactly 1 is still not enough. A lower price only shrinks the top of the fraction. It does not reduce the token balance.

## Which market this was

The contracts were the real Aave V3 Sepolia contracts at that historical block. The writes stayed on the local copy. `POOL_REVISION = 1`. This deployment is **V3.0**, not the later V3.3.

The collateral is Aave’s own test WETH, <Addr a="0xC558DBdd856501FCd9aaF1E62eae57A9F0629a3c" short />. The borrowed token is Aave test USDC, <Addr a="0x94a9D9AC8a22534E3FaCa9F4e7F2E2cf85d5E4C8" short />. These are not the Uniswap WETH and Circle USDC from Chapter 16. The names match. The addresses do not. Chapter 16’s USDC cannot repay this market.

At that block, WETH can be collateral and cannot be borrowed. Its <Term id="ltv">loan-to-value ratio</Term> is 80%. Its <Term id="liquidation-threshold">liquidation threshold</Term> is 82.5%. The USDC oracle price is 1. The original WETH price source stores a fixed answer, 4,000.00000000. It has no function that changes the price. It is a test constant, not a live quote anyone can dial.

In the local run, an operator deployed a stand-in price source. A local impersonation of the pool admin pointed WETH at that source. The price was still 4,000, and USDC was still 1. Only then did the run lower the WETH quote. The script did not write Aave’s collateral or debt storage. An ordinary user does not have that admin role on the public network.

## Open the position: 1 WETH, borrow 2,500

The faucet cannot mint this WETH. A simulated mint reverts with `not mintable`. The account wraps 1 ETH with `deposit`, approves the pool for exactly that amount, supplies it, and borrows 2,500 test USDC.

One 12-second local block later, the debt is not a clean 2,500. The reading is **2,500.000610**. The health factor is about **1.32**. Collateral is still **1** WETH.

This test pool is heavily used. The variable borrow rate, in ray units, is `639970093802817705403628356`, about **64%** a year. Each block adds a visible crumb of interest. A napkin formula that ignores interest misses the protocol by a little. That interest is the main gap. It is not a hidden penalty.

## Loan-to-value is not the liquidation switch

The 80% ratio limits new borrowing. One WETH at a value of 4,000 gives a borrowing room of 3,200. The lab borrowed 2,500, so room remained.

The 82.5% ratio enters the health factor. One times 4,000 times 0.825, divided by 2,500.000610, gives the 1.32 reading above. The 2.5 point gap is a buffer. It stops a loan that was just filled from becoming liquidatable after a tiny price move.

If the position already has debt, a withdrawal must still leave the position acceptable. Do not treat the loan-to-value ratio as a general withdrawal line.

## At 2,800 the watch is still in the cupboard

After the local WETH quote becomes 2,800, and before anyone liquidates:

- The aWETH balance is still **1.000000000000000000**.
- The debt is **2,500.001218**. One more block added only interest.
- The health factor is about **0.924**. It is below 1, and also below 0.95.

The valuation moved from 4,000 to 2,800. The balance did not lose one wei. Collateral moves in the next step, when someone calls liquidation.

## After that call, assets move

<LiqFlow />

At the moment of liquidation, the repaid debt is **2,500.002436** test USDC. Interest grew again after the earlier reading. The health factor is at or below 0.95, so this deployment allows one call to cover **100%**. The request said “maximum”. The protocol cut it to the full debt.

The borrower loses **0.9375009135** WETH. Of that, **0.933036623435714286** goes to the liquidator. **0.004464290064285714** goes to the protocol treasury as a deposit receipt. At the 2,800 oracle mark, the 5% liquidation bonus is about 112.50 for the liquidator and about 12.50 for the protocol. The protocol’s share is 10% of the bonus, not 10% of the collateral, and not the flash-loan fee split in the next chapter. That mark is a test-token valuation, not dollar income. The borrower still holds the 2,500 test USDC already borrowed, plus about 0.0624990865 WETH of deposit receipt.

The same transaction also shows a mint to the treasury. That mint records interest the treasury had **already** accrued. It is not an extra bonus. The treasury’s balance rises by exactly the protocol fee.

## Repay 750, and the same price is safe

Restore the same open snapshot. Repay **750** test USDC. The debt becomes **1,750.001826**. At a price of 4,000 the health factor is **1.885712318108175505**. Then set the same price, 2,800.

The debt is now **1,750.002252**. The health factor is still about **1.32**, above 1. The price is the same 2,800, and collateral is still 1 WETH. The liquidation call reverts with error **45**.

Why is this so close to the opening 1.32? Repaying about 30% of the debt offsets the 30% drop from 4,000 to 2,800. The price matches. The debt does not. The ratio changes.

The later full repayment transfers **1,750.004383**. The two repayments exceed the original 2,500 principal by **0.004383** test USDC. That is the interest across these blocks. The account was short by that crumb, so it minted 1 test USDC from the faucet. After closing, it withdraws exactly 1 WETH and the allowances are zero. With no debt, the protocol reports a huge sentinel health factor, not zero.

## Calculate the boundary with the right percentage

Set the health factor to 1:

**boundary price ≈ debt ÷ (collateral amount × 0.825)**

With the opening debt 2,500.000610, that price is about **3,030.30376970**. A read-only probe on the same block finds the edge. A price at or below **3,030.30376969** can be liquidated. At **3,030.30376970** the health factor is exactly 1, so it **cannot** be liquidated. The probe matches the on-copy reading.

Using the 80% loan-to-value ratio instead gives 3,125. That is the price where you can no longer borrow as much. It is not the liquidation price.

Interest slowly lifts the boundary. About every 12 seconds, a 2,500 debt grows by about 0.000608, and the boundary moves up by about 0.00074. A principal-only figure of 3,030.30303030 sits about 0.00074 below the protocol’s opening boundary.

After the 750 repayment, the highest liquidatable price is **2,121.21433453**. The price 2,800 is still far above that line.

## 50% and 100% are caps, and this is V3.0

The <Term id="close-factor">close factor</Term> is a maximum, not an amount the protocol always takes. On this V3.0 deployment:

- At a health factor of 1 or more, liquidation is refused.
- Above 0.95 and below 1, one call may cover at most 50%.
- At or below 0.95, one call may cover at most 100%.

This deployment does **not** have the later V3.3 rule that lets a small debt be closed in full. The 100% cap here exists only because the health factor is at or below 0.95. The debt size of 2,500 is not the reason.

A control uses the same snapshot and a price of **2,950**. The health factor is about **0.973**, between 0.95 and 1. A maximum request repays only **1,250.001218**. The borrower loses **0.444915687762711864** WETH. Afterwards the health factor is about **1.08**. The remaining debt is 2 minimal units under a perfect half. That is index rounding, not an extra fine.

## In short

- The health factor is collateral value times the liquidation threshold, divided by debt. The loan-to-value ratio only limits new borrowing.
- A price drop changes the valuation. Collateral moves when someone calls liquidation.
- At the same price, 2,800, the unpaid position can be closed in one call. After repaying 750, error 45 refuses the call.
- The boundary uses 82.5% and is about 3,030.30, not the 3,125 you get from 80%. A health factor of exactly 1 is not liquidatable.
- This deployment is V3.0. The caps on either side of 0.95 are 50% and 100%. There is no V3.3 small-debt rule.
- The 64% test rate adds debt every block. Cite Chapter 18’s readings only in Chapter 18.
