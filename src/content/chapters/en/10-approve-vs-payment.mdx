<p class="lead">Three roles: holder, spender, recipient. Approving and spending are two separate actions.</p>

## Why do wallets keep asking you to "approve" rather than "pay"?

Many decentralised exchanges and services first ask the holder to **approve** a smart contract to use up to some amount of a token (`approve`), and only later, when you act, does that contract call `transferFrom`.

- **`approve` is not a payment**: it only writes into the token contract "B may move up to this much of A's tokens". **Balances do not change at all.**
- What actually moves balances is a later `transferFrom` (move) or `burnFrom` (destroy) sent by the spender.
- An approval **stays valid** until the allowance is used up or the holder sends another `approve` to change it.

So although an approval is not "money already gone", a long-lived approval is a standing permission for future deductions.

## An allowance is an overwritable "remaining amount"

- After part of it is used, the rest **stays valid**: in the lab B burned 100 and 400 remained.
- A new `approve` **overwrites; it does not add**. If the allowance is 100 and you `approve(B, 500)`, the result is 500, not 600.
- To clear it, send `approve(B, 0)`.

## Three risks and one habit

1. **Too large an allowance**: if the approved contract has a bug or malicious logic, a bigger allowance means a bigger possible loss.
2. **Approving the wrong address**: a familiar name does not make an address trustworthy (remember Chapter 9: names are not unique).
3. **Forgotten approvals**: after you stop using a service, the allowance may still be usable.

**One habit**: at the end of every experiment, check `allowance`; when it is no longer needed, send `approve(spender, 0)` to revoke it.

<Callout type="warn" title="Revoking has a time window too">

A revocation must be sent by the **holder**, and takes effect only once it is mined. Until then, the old allowance can still be used.

</Callout>

## Everyday analogy

You authorise a food-delivery platform to deduct "up to 100 loyalty points a month". Nothing is deducted at that moment; points are only taken when the platform actually places an order. Later you stop using it and set the deductible limit to 0. That withdraws permission; it does not hand 100 points to anyone.
