<p class="lead"><Term id="burn">Burning</Term> really lowers <Term id="total-supply">total supply</Term>; revoking an allowance changes only the allowance, never balances.</p>

## Deploying a burnable token

The deployed MBox has no burn function and cannot get one later, so a separate <Term id="token">token</Term> with `burn` / `burnFrom` was deployed:

- Contract <Term id="address">address</Term>: <Addr a="0x2AE6BbE8b524381535D1f443fe95018b1f64e3F2" />
- Name: `超级无敌宇宙第一老马斯克看了都不知道该叫什么的焚烧Token` (the MBox name with "焚烧", "burn", added), symbol `MBox-Burn`, initial supply 1,000,000, all to account A

Right after deployment, A burned 1,000 of its own tokens: `totalSupply` fell from 1,000,000 to **999,000**. The old MBox contract was not touched.

## burn is not "transfer to 0x0"

- **`burn`** lowers **both** the holder's balance and `totalSupply`, and emits `Transfer(holder → 0x0, amount)`.
- **A plain `transfer` to the <Term id="zero-address">zero address</Term>** (if a contract allows it) changes balances only and does **not** lower `totalSupply`.

Both can show a "transfer to 0x0" <Term id="event">event</Term> in an <Term id="block-explorer">explorer</Term>. Whether supply actually fell must be read from `totalSupply()`, not inferred from the event. This lab used a dedicated `burn`, so supply really went down.

## approve 500, burnFrom 100

1. A first sent B **0.0001 <Term id="sepeth">SepETH</Term>** to pay for test <Term id="gas">gas</Term> (B never used a <Term id="faucet">faucet</Term>).
2. A called `approve(B, 500)`: <Term id="allowance">allowance</Term> 500, balances unchanged.
3. B called `burnFrom(A, 100)`: A lost 100, supply fell another 100 to **998,900**, and **400** of the allowance remained.

Two `transferFrom` calls in Chapter 12 later used 300 of those 400, **leaving 100 at that point**. D-01 below subsequently revoked that leftover allowance; it was 0 at the snapshot.

## D-01: revoking the leftover 100

The Phase-0 review recorded this leftover allowance as a Low finding (F-ALLOW-001): not a compromised contract but a leftover from the experiment. If B <Term id="signature">signed</Term>, it could still move up to 100 test tokens. Decision **D-01** approved revoking it. After re-reading the allowance, <Term id="nonce">nonce</Term> and balances and <Term id="simulate">simulating</Term> again, A <Term id="broadcast">broadcast</Term> **exactly one** `approve(B, 0)`:

<Tx h="0x4571e581958308c820ab16da26aa9d1a2622caa7c720ebe164b5d2c29a3747f2" />

- Block **12117312**, status success; gasUsed 24,538, effective gas price 69 <Term id="wei">wei</Term>, fee 1,693,122 wei
- `allowance(A, B)`: **100 → 0**
- Balances and supply **unchanged**: A 998,800, B 100, `totalSupply` 998,900
- A's nonce 19 → **20**

## Three things you can check yourself

- **Transaction details**: the network is <Term id="sepolia">Sepolia</Term>, the sender is A, the target is the MBox-Burn contract, the status is success.
- **Event or contract read**: the `Approval` event, or read `allowance(A, B)` directly and see that it is 0.
- **Balances and supply**: an <Term id="approve">approval</Term> changes only the allowance and **should not** change the holder's assets.

<Callout type="note" title="Least privilege">

The core security lesson here is "least necessary privilege". It often matters more than piling ever more features into a token contract.

</Callout>
